# Rawhide — What's New (Nightlies)

These notes feed the in-app "Update Available" dialog for Rawhide / cutting-edge builds.

## Recent improvements (unreleased — ships in the next build)

- 🍽️ **Fixed: needs could crater 35-40 points in one message** — Someone would be fine, then one reply later be starving, exhausted or desperate for a bathroom, sometimes several at once. It was a feedback loop: the app tells the AI how the character feels ("sharp, gnawing hunger cramps"), the AI writes her clutching her stomach — and then the part that scores the scene read *that* and decided she'd got hungrier still. The worse she felt, the more vividly it was written, the harder the next hit. **Describing a feeling is no longer counted as the feeling getting worse.** What a single scene can take out of a need is now capped per need, and the caps are deliberately different: drinking a soda still hits her bladder about three times harder than a normal turn would, a soaking still ruins her hygiene, but nothing drops off a cliff any more. Eating, sleeping, bathing and using the bathroom still fill her right up in one go, exactly as before. The Needs strength slider still widens everything, and every setting is calmer than it was.

- 🔌 **Big fix: several features did nothing at all unless the Realism Engine was on** — Pockets & Wardrobe, Likes & Dislikes, Ambitions, Promises, the "you've been away" note and the story clock's own time and weather all travel to the AI inside one shared block of text — and that whole block was being skipped whenever the Realism Engine was off. Every one of those features says plainly in Settings that it doesn't need the engine, and their switches, sidebars and displays all worked, so there was nothing to see. The AI just never got told. Worst hit: **Pockets & Wardrobe**, which spends an AI request every turn — with the engine off you were paying for it and the character was never told what she was wearing — and the **story clock's stand-alone mode**, which exists purely to keep time running without the engine and was doing so entirely in private. All of them reach the AI now. Nothing changes if you run with the engine on.

- 🎭 **Fixed: characters you downloaded ignored your Settings defaults** — Turn on Realism, Afterglow or Chaos Mode as a global default, open a character card you'd downloaded from Chub (or exported from another app), and none of it applied. The app was only reading your defaults for characters that already had Front Porch settings saved on them — which downloaded cards, by definition, don't. That was backwards: those are exactly the cards the defaults exist for. Your own characters are unaffected.

- 🧥 **Group chats now show what each character is wearing and carrying** — Pockets & Wardrobe worked in group chats the whole time — the AI knew, the phone/web app showed it — but the desktop sidebar only ever displayed it in one-to-one chats, so you couldn't see it and couldn't correct a wrong entry. Each member's card now shows their wardrobe with the same tap-to-remove as a solo chat.

- 🔎 **New: you can turn off "notice new characters"** — Every few messages the app checks whether a new named character has appeared in the story and offers to bring them in. Handy, and easy to find intrusive. There's now a switch for it in **Settings → Porch Life → Notice new characters**, on by default. Turn it off and it stops asking — you can still invite someone in yourself whenever you like. On the web/mobile app too.

- 🎲 **Fixed: starting a new chat inside a group ignored your Chaos Mode default** — Changing the global Chaos setting while a group was open, then starting a fresh chat in it, kept the old setting. One-to-one chats always got this right.

- 👗 **Fixed: a character's wardrobe was invisible in the sidebar until you sent your first message** — Dress a character, open a chat with her, and the Pockets & Wardrobe section simply wasn't there. It appeared only after you typed something — which is the worst possible timing, because opening the chat is exactly when you go to check the wardrobe saved. (It did save; it just wasn't being shown yet.) She now arrives dressed, on the greeting, in one-to-one chats and group chats alike.

- 🧥 **Fixed: wardrobe and pocket items you typed onto a character were thrown away when you pressed Save** — Dressing a character in the editor — what she's wearing, what she's carrying, and her ambitions, likes and dislikes too — saved successfully, wrote the file, and lost every word of it. There was no error and nothing looked wrong; reopen the character and the chips were simply gone. The save only wrote that part of the card if the Realism Engine was on for that character or you'd touched one of the engine's own sliders, and none of those boxes count as either. It now saves whenever you've actually written something, which is the only rule that can't be forgotten the next time a box is added.

- 🎲 **Chaos Mode has a global switch at last** — Chaos could only ever be turned on one chat at a time, forever, and the bottom of the Porch Life tab had a little note apologising for it. It's now a row like everything else in **Settings → Porch Life**, it depends on nothing, and it's off unless you turn it on. Switch it on and new chats and groups start with Chaos running; any single chat can still overrule it from its sidebar, same as before. The apologetic note is gone. On the web/mobile app too.

- 💗 **Characters act on what they're into, instead of just quietly having preferences** — The 18+ tastes you write on a card were only ever a footnote in the AI's instructions: "relevant when the scene is already there." It said *when* they applied and never what to do about them, so a character never once asked for anything or turned anything down — while the engine was already, silently, moving bond and trust based on whether a scene hit them. She was rewarding and penalising you over things she'd never say out loud. Now she can say them: she asks for what she wants **in her own voice** — a dominant character presses, a soft-spoken one hints or waits to be noticed — and she declines what she isn't interested in rather than going along with it. Refuse something she wanted and it shows in her mood afterwards, sharper or quieter as fits who she is, and the engine scores that refusal as the real moment it is. New switch: **Settings → Porch Life → After Dark → Acts on desires**, off unless you turn it on, and it needs the Realism Engine because that's what judges the answer she gets. **Costs nothing** — no extra AI request, just two more lines in the prompt.

- 🌧️ **Clothes react to what YOU describe, not just what she says** — A character in a red sundress who steps out into the rain should end up in a rain-soaked red sundress; chain mail should come out of a fight dented. The app could always record that, but it was only ever shown the character's own reply — so if *you* wrote the rain and she answered "wait for me!", nothing in what it read mentioned weather and the dress stayed dry. It now sees the last few messages of the exchange, so a change you narrate counts too. It also got better examples to work from — weather, wear and damage, not just food — which matters most on smaller local models.

- 🧠 **Characters should make more sense from here on** — The block of context the AI gets before every reply — the time, the weather, how she feels about you, what she wants, what she's carrying, how she's doing — was built up one piece at a time over months and nobody had ever read it start to finish. It showed. It told her she was starving four lines before mentioning the food in her pocket. It said where she was standing *last*, after everything she might do from there. It said "she came in having a rough day" before any of the reasons why. It's been rewritten in the order a person would actually think it: where we are, who you are to her, what she promised, who she is, then how she's doing right now. Nothing was added or removed — it just makes sense now. You'll notice it most on smaller local models, which had the hardest time with the old arrangement.

- 🍫 **Characters use what's in their pockets now** — If she's starving and there's a candy bar in her pocket, she should eat the candy bar — not announce she's hungry and head off to cook something. Both facts were already in front of the AI; nothing told it to put them together, so whether she noticed came down to how clever the model was. Now, when a character genuinely needs something **and** is carrying or wearing something, she's told she'd reach for what she has before looking elsewhere. It works for more than food — a coat when she's cold, a drink when she's flagging. She still *shows* how she feels; she just stops overlooking what's in her own hand. **Costs nothing** — no extra AI request, one short line. Needs **Needs** and **Pockets & Wardrobe** both switched on, since it has nothing to join otherwise.

- 🧹 **Fixed: the Pockets panel stayed in the sidebar after you turned Pockets off** — Switching **Pockets & Wardrobe** off in **Settings → Porch Life** stopped the tracking, but any chat that had already been using it kept showing the wearing/carrying panel in the sidebar — a leftover from a feature that was no longer running. Off now means off everywhere: the panel disappears with the switch, exactly as it does on the web app, which had it right all along. **Nothing is thrown away** — turn Pockets back on and everything she was carrying is still there, condition and all.

- 👕 **Fixed: a character's starting outfit didn't reach her first reply** — If you gave a character something to start with, she didn't actually know about it when she answered your first message. The app only wrote down what she had *after* a reply, so the apron you dressed her in existed from her second reply onward and the opening exchange was written as though she had nothing. The chat sidebar was empty for just as long, which made it look like the wardrobe hadn't saved. Her starting things are now in place before she says a word — visible in the sidebar the moment you open the chat, and in her very first reply. Works the same in group chats, where each character starts with their own.

- 🎒 **The Stoop shows what a character comes with** — Browsing a character on The Stoop already told you their ambitions and what they're drawn to or put off by. Now it also shows their starting **Pockets & Wardrobe** — what they're wearing and carrying before the first message, condition and all — so you know what you're getting before you download. It's on the group-member cards too, and on the web/mobile app. (Also fixed while we were in there: a really long line on a card's panel used to render as a chunk of the app's own source code instead of the text.)

- 🧥 **Give your characters something to start with** — Pockets & Wardrobe could always track what a character is wearing and carrying, but there was nowhere to say what they OWN before the first message — unless you were willing to hand-edit the character file. Now there's a **Pockets & Wardrobe** box in the character editor and in both character creators: type what they're **Wearing** and what they're **Carrying**, one thing per chip. Add a condition in brackets — "sundress (rain-soaked)", "pocket knife (blunt)", "a candy bar (half-eaten)" — and the story picks up from there and keeps it current. Up to eight of each. It's on the character, so it travels with them: share them to The Stoop and whoever downloads them gets the same starting kit. On the web/mobile editor too. (Nothing changes for characters you don't give a wardrobe to, and the items only start being tracked once **Pockets & Wardrobe** is switched on in **Settings → Porch Life**.)

- 📱 **Fixed: editing a character from your phone erased the things they started with** — If a character you downloaded arrived already holding something — a locket, a set of keys, a coat — that came from their card, and the web/mobile editor threw it away. Not on a field you'd touched: any save at all, even a one-word tweak to their description, silently dropped it, and nothing on the page ever mentioned an inventory so there was no reason to suspect it. Editing from the desktop app was never affected. It's carried through properly now, condition and all, and it stays put whether you edit from the desktop, a phone, or a browser.

- 🧥 **Fixed: a one-to-one chat forgot everything in her pockets when you closed it** — Pockets & Wardrobe kept a proper record while the chat was open, but in a one-to-one conversation that record only ever lived in memory. Close the chat, reopen it, and she was empty-handed again — the keys gone, the coat back on, everything you'd watched her pick up quietly reset to whatever her character card started with. Group chats were never affected, which is why this could go unnoticed. Her pockets are now saved with the conversation and come back exactly as they were, condition and all: still rain-soaked, still half-eaten. Two related leaks went with it — starting a **new** chat no longer finds her still holding the last conversation's things, and when a group shrinks back down to a one-to-one the last character standing keeps what she was carrying instead of walking out empty-handed. Existing chats simply start recording from now on.

- 🔁 **Characters can hand things to each other now** — With Pockets & Wardrobe on, one character handing something to another used to be half a story: it left her hands and reached nobody, so the coat she passed you never showed up in anyone's pocket. Turn on **Hand things between characters** in **Settings → Porch Life** and it actually moves — into the other character's pockets, still muddy or still torn or still half-eaten, exactly as it was. It costs nothing extra; it rides the check Pockets already does every reply. **Best with a frontier model** (Claude, GPT, Gemini): working out *who* received something is a harder question than noticing what changed, and smaller local models often name the wrong person. So the app only accepts a name that clearly matches somebody in the chat — if it isn't sure, it refuses to guess, and the item leaves the giver and goes nowhere, exactly as it did before. Group chats only, since in a one-to-one the only other person is you.

- 🧰 **Pockets & Wardrobe now uses the fast, reliable path it was built for** — Modern models can answer a question by filling in a form instead of writing JSON by hand, which is far less likely to come back garbled. Pockets was asking for that every turn and then throwing the answer away, so it silently fell back to the older text method from the day it shipped — a little slower and a little more fragile than it needed to be. It uses the form now. Nothing changes about what it tracks; it should just miss fewer changes on smaller local models.

- 🔥 **Fixed: Afterglow did nothing for most people who turned it on** — If you switched on Afterglow and desire just climbed to the top and stayed pinned there forever, this is why. Afterglow starts when a character reaches a climax — and the thing that noticed climaxes was buried inside the **needs simulation**, which most characters have switched off. So on most characters nothing was ever detected, nothing ever started, and the setting sat there looking perfectly enabled because the one thing it said it needed — the Realism Engine — really was on. Noticing a climax now stands on its own instead of being bundled into the needs simulation. **Afterglow needs the Realism Engine and nothing else**, exactly as the switch always said. Nothing to change on your end — turn it on and it works. One trade-off worth knowing: standing on its own costs one short extra AI request per reply while Afterglow is on, so it is a little slower and costs a little more on a paid API.

- 🌤️ **Characters can now have a bad day that has nothing to do with you** — Every mood swing in the app was caused by something you said. That sounds right until you notice what it means: you are the only thing in her world, and every flicker of feeling is a verdict on you. Turn on **Standing Mood** in **Settings → Porch Life** and she can walk in already tired, hungry, worn down by a week of rain — or cheerful because she slept well and it is a beautiful day. **You can always tell which is which.** Hover the mood chip under any reply and it tells you exactly what she came in carrying ("Came in not at her best — they are exhausted"), and the chat sidebar shows it too. It is never made up: it comes from things the app already tracks — how she has slept and eaten, the hour, the weather — and it is deliberately small, so anything you actually do still matters more than the kind of day she has had. It never touches bond or trust; a rough night can colour her tone, but it can't damage what is between you. **Costs nothing** — no extra AI request, ever.

- 💬 **The mood chip finally explains itself** — Every other chip under a reply told you why: bond and trust have always had a reason on hover, so do needs, the Director and Chance Time. "Mood: wistful" was the one that never did, because nothing in the app knew the answer. With Standing Mood on, it does now.

- 🧥 **Characters remember what they're wearing and carrying** — Nothing in an AI chat keeps track of this, so it's always been the same story: she picks up the car keys, and forty messages later they've scrolled out of the conversation and simply stopped existing. She's barefoot in a scene where she put boots on. Turn on **Pockets & Wardrobe** in **Settings → Porch Life** and there's a real record instead: the keys stay in her pocket, the coat she took off stays off, and things change as they're used — a candy bar becomes a wrapper, a sword gets notched, a hem gets rain-soaked. You'll see what changed under each reply, and the chat sidebar lists everything she's wearing and carrying, with an × to strike anything out if the AI got it wrong. Characters you download can arrive already holding things, if their author set that up — and you can set it up yourself now, on your own characters (see below). It's **off unless you turn it on**, because it costs one short extra AI request per reply and that's your call. Kept per chat and cleared with it — nothing follows her into another conversation. On the web/mobile app too.

- ♥ **Characters can be given likes and dislikes — and they actually act on them** — Until now the only way to say what a character was drawn to was to write it into their personality paragraph and hope it stuck. Thirty messages later it had scrolled out of the AI's memory. There's now a proper **Likes & Dislikes** section in the character editor and both creators: one short phrase per chip, *Drawn to* and *Put off by*. Give her "thunderstorms" and she lights up when it rains; give her "being interrupted" and she bristles when you cut her off — and you don't have to write a word of it into her description. With the Realism Engine on it goes further: how much a moment moves her bond and trust is now weighed against what *she* cares about, so the same gesture can warm one character and annoy another. Without the engine she still acts on them — reacting to what she likes was never something that needed bond tracking. If you use 18+ themes there's a separate, private **Intimate preferences** section that only appears once you've turned 18+ themes on, and stays out of the AI's instructions entirely otherwise. It's on the web app too, and it travels with shared characters — you'll see a character's likes and dislikes on their Stoop page before you download them.

- 🧭 **You can finally see which goal a quest is working toward** — Characters had started picking quests that climb their long-term ambitions, but nothing on screen ever said so, which made it look like they were still inventing errands at random. Now every quest shows the ambition it's a step toward, right under the goal — in the chat sidebar, in the group objectives window, and on the web/mobile app. Each ambition returns the favour: under its progress bar you'll see the quest she's actually working on right now, instead of a bar that only twitches when something finishes. Quests that serve no ambition simply don't show a tag — a character who chases something for no reason at all is a person, not a plot device.

- 🗺️ **The chat sidebar now tells you where a starting quest goes** — When "Current Task / Quest" left the character editor, the box you'd type it into moved to the Objectives panel in the chat sidebar — but nothing there said so. A fresh chat just showed an unlabelled "Add new goal…" field under an empty list. It now opens with **Starting quest** and a line explaining you can set one or leave it and let her come up with her own. (The web app already did this; desktop had fallen behind.)

- 🧭 **Character pages on The Stoop show their ambitions** — Browsing a shared character, you could read their description, personality and greeting but never the long-term goals that drive everything they do. Ambitions now have their own section on the card page, on desktop and on the hub — for every member of a shared group chat too.

- 🏔️ **Characters now take on quests that actually lead somewhere** — Ambitions were a wish list the story never consulted. A character could want to open her own bakery for fifty chats while every quest she invented for herself was about something else entirely, and the only time the ambition was ever checked was after a quest finished, to ask whether it had happened to help. Now it works the way you'd expect: when a character decides on a new goal, she's thinking about what she's actually working toward and picks the next real step up that hill. Life still happens — she can still want something that has nothing to do with any of it, because a character whose every wish serves the plot reads like a questgiver rather than a person. Each quest also remembers which ambition it belongs to, so finishing one credits the right goal instead of the app guessing after the fact. Characters with no ambitions are completely unaffected, down to the cost of a turn.

- 🗺️ **"Current Task / Quest" has left the character editor — and nothing you typed there is lost** — That box asked you to bake a single errand ("find the missing artifact") permanently into a character, right next to Ambitions, which asks for the same kind of sentence. Two boxes, one question, and the wrong answer stuck to the card forever. Ambitions now stands alone: the long-term goals that follow a character into every chat. A quest belongs to *one* conversation, so that's where it lives — the Objectives panel in the chat sidebar, where you could always add, edit and finish them. **If any of your characters already had a task typed in, it's still on the card and it still becomes their starting quest the moment a new chat opens** — including in group chats, where it previously did nothing at all. Same on the web/mobile app.

- 🚩 **Ambitions are a real editor now — and you can set them while creating a character** — A character's long-term goals were edited in a small "one per line" text box buried under the System Prompt, which is to say most people never found it. Ambitions now get their own proper section with one goal per chip, up near the rest of the personality, in **both** the character editor and the character creator — the creator had no way to set them at all before. Blank lines, stray spaces and accidental duplicates simply can't happen any more, and you can see at a glance how many you have. On the web app too.

- 🔞 **Adult switches are now hidden unless you ask for them** — The Afterglow setting sat in the middle of the main feature list where everyone saw it, whether or not they use 18+ themes at all. There's now a plain **18+ themes** switch in **Settings → General**, and the adult features live in their own **After Dark** group that simply isn't there when it's off. If you already had Afterglow turned on, nothing changes and nothing is hidden from you — the app takes that as your answer. Turning it off never erases what you set; it only hides the switches.

- 🌱 **Growth Rings has a switch in Porch Life at last** — Slow character evolution was already a real setting, but the only way to reach it was the chat sidebar, and the Porch Life tab wrongly said it was per-chat only. It's now a row like everything else, on desktop and the web app.

- 👋 **Fixed: "you've been away a while" never actually reached the character** — If you turned on the option for a character to notice you'd been gone, the note telling them so was tucked inside the story-clock line — so whenever the clock wasn't running, the note vanished with it. The feature saved, confirmed, and did nothing. It now stands on its own and works whenever you've switched it on, clock or no clock.

- 🤝 **Promises no longer move trust and bond while the Realism Engine is off** — Keeping or breaking a promise nudged those scores even in chats where you'd turned the engine off and the sidebar plainly said nothing was being tracked. Promises still work completely — they're detected, logged, marked kept or broken, and remembered — they just stop quietly editing numbers you asked the app not to keep.

- 🎯 **Objectives can finally be switched off — and Ambitions no longer hide** — Quests quietly checked in with the AI as your story moved, forever, with no way to stop it: there was no Objectives switch anywhere in the app. Now there is one, in **Settings → Porch Life**, and it depends on nothing else — turn it off and the checking stops, the character stops inventing new goals, and the Objectives panel leaves the chat sidebar rather than sitting there looking busy. **Your quests and tasks are kept** — flip it back on and everything is exactly where you left it. Ambitions now sit under Objectives where they belong, since finishing a quest is the only thing that nudges a long-term goal forward. Two related fixes: turning the Realism Engine off used to **hide** the Ambitions row in the sidebar even though ambitions were still quietly working, and switching Ambitions off hid the display while still spending an AI request every time a quest completed. Both fixed. On the web/mobile app too.

- ⏰ **The story clock can now keep time without the Realism Engine** — Turning the Realism Engine off used to freeze the story's clock solid: no dawn, no evening, no Day 2, and no weather or dreams either, because those follow the calendar. That was never really about bonds and trust — the clock just needs the AI to judge how long each exchange took. So now it can do that on its own. In **Settings → Porch Life → Passage of Time**, with the engine off you'll find a new switch: *Keep the clock running without the engine*. Flip it and time moves properly again — a shared meal advances the clock further than a passing hello — and weather and dreams come back with it. It's **off unless you turn it on**, because it costs one short extra AI call per turn and that's your call to make, not ours. With the Realism Engine on, nothing changes at all: the clock rides along with work the engine was already doing, free as always. Also fixed while we were in there: "(OOC: skip to morning)" was quietly ignored whenever the engine was off, and now works.

- 🧹 **Big internal cleanup COMPLETE: every oversized file in the app is gone** — What began as twenty-six enormous, tangled files — including the story reader and dashboard, the Worlds page, the persona page, the group editor, the voice and appearance settings, the chat message bubble itself (every reply you see), the engines that track bonds, trust and feelings, the voice system, the character library, the character creator's brain, the Porch Stories writer, the image generator, the engine that writes every single reply, the app's startup sequence, the central conversation brain that coordinates everything, and even the database layer itself (proven safe by rebuilding its machine-generated schema file and confirming it byte-for-byte identical — your data is untouched by definition) — is now clean, small, focused pieces that are far safer to fix and extend. Every moved line was verified against the original, so nothing about how anything behaves changes. Before each engine was touched, new tests were written that pin down exactly how it works today (and each test was deliberately broken once to prove it actually catches problems). The sweep also caught real bugs along the way (two voice-settings dropdowns that clipped long names), and several screens joined the app's warm amber look, dropping stray cool-blue and purple accents. The story reader's book keeps its proper paper-and-leather look in every theme, on purpose.

- 👆 **Fixed: tapping rows in a Stoop card's detail panel gave no visual feedback** — The little ripple you expect when tapping the greeting and lore rows was being painted where you couldn't see it, ever since the panel first shipped. It shows now.

- 🏡 **New Settings tab: Porch Life — every "feels alive" switch in one place** — The Realism Engine, needs, the story clock, weather, the Journal, dreams, promises, ambitions and the welcome-back recap all used to hide inside one card on the General tab, tucked under the Realism switch — so turning Realism off literally **hid the switches** for features that work fine without it. They now live in their own tab, grouped by what they are, and each one says plainly what it needs. Anything that genuinely can't run on its own is greyed out until you turn on what it depends on, instead of pretending to work. Needs got a global switch for the first time (it was only ever per-chat), and the mouthful "NSFW Cooldown" is now **Afterglow**. On the web/mobile app too.
