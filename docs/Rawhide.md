# Rawhide — What's New (Nightlies)

These notes feed the in-app "Update Available" dialog for Rawhide / cutting-edge builds.

## Recent improvements (unreleased — ships in the next build)

- 🧠 **Memory stops being a black box — and stops arguing with the story recap** — Two changes to Memory (RAG), both visible. First: the sidebar Memory panel now shows a **receipt for every reply** — how many past moments were searched, how many were woven into the reply, and which ones, each with the story day it came from; tap a line and the chat jumps straight to the original message. If nothing needed looking up, it says so instead of leaving you guessing. The same receipt appears in the web app's Memory section. Second, a real fix underneath: retrieved lines used to arrive with no sense of *when* — a "I never want to see you again" from Day 2 read exactly like it was said just now, even though the story recap knew they'd made up since, and the AI would waste its reply trying to reconcile the two. Every remembered line is now stamped with its story day (or marked as coming from another chat) and presented oldest-first, so old words stay old. Memories shared through a photo now get found too — the part that searches your history finally sees the "[shared a photo]" note the rest of the app already saw.

- 🌙 **Morning messages stop waiting on last night's dream** — When a story crosses a night, the character dreams, and that dream is written by the AI. Until now it was written at the worst possible moment: the instant you sent your first message of the new morning, which sat frozen while a whole extra AI request ran. The dream is now written quietly in the background at the end of the previous evening's exchange — the app already knows a night has passed by then — so when your morning message arrives, the dream is simply there, already finished, in exactly the same place it always appeared. Same dreams, same journal entry, none of the waiting.

- 📷 **All the background judges can see your photos now** — When you share a photo in chat, the AI is told about it ("shared a photo: a sunset over the bay") so your character can react. The judges that score bond, trust and mood got that note — but the ones that track needs, Afterglow and Pockets & Wardrobe were reading a version of the conversation where the photo simply didn't exist, so a turn that was *about* the photo looked to them like you'd said nothing at all. They all read the same conversation now.

- 💗 **Lust could move twice in one turn — fixed** — With NSFW cooldowns on, each turn's arousal change is supposed to be scored once, by the judge whose job it is. But if the bond-and-trust judge happened to volunteer an arousal number too (some models are chatty like that), the app applied *both* — Lust moved by the sum while the little chip on the message showed only one of them, and whether it happened at all depended on the model's mood that turn. One judge owns that number now; a volunteered duplicate is ignored.

- 🍽️ **A dead safety check on hunger is back on duty** — The optional Realism Director reviews the judges' scores and reins in ones the scene doesn't support — "she got 20 points hungrier" in a scene where nobody ate, say. The hunger check had been looking for its number under a name the judges never actually use, so it had never once fired on real output. It reads the right name now, and there's a test that fails loudly if it ever goes quiet again.

- ⚡ **One-Shot Eval now has an Auto mode — and it's the new default** — The Realism Engine can score each scene with one combined AI request instead of four separate ones, which is faster and cheaper — but small local models sometimes fumble the combined question, which is why it's been an off-by-default experiment until now. The switch is now three-way: **Auto** (new default) uses the single combined request on remote AI services that support tool calls — where it works reliably — and automatically keeps the safer four-request path on local models; **On** and **Off** force it either way, and your choice always wins. If you'd already turned One-Shot on, you stay on. In the chat sidebar under Character State, and on the web/mobile app too.

- ⚡ **The Realism Engine's judges stopped re-reading the character sheet three times a turn** — Every turn, three background judges score the scene (bond and trust, mood, story direction), and each was being handed its own full copy of the character's personality, your relationship standing, and her tastes — the same text, three times, and because each copy *started* differently, a local AI had to re-read all of it from scratch for every judge. The three now share one identical opening, so the AI reads it once and skips straight to each judge's actual question. On local models this cuts the engine's per-turn reading work roughly in half; nothing about the scoring itself changed.

- ⚡ **Replies start faster when a quest is open** — With the Realism Engine on, the app was quietly asking the AI "did she finish any of her quest steps?" before **every single reply**, the whole time any quest was open — a full extra AI request you sat through even when the conversation was nowhere near the quest. It now asks on the schedule each quest already shows in its panel, plus immediately whenever the scene actually touches the quest (mention the lighthouse and the lighthouse quest gets checked right away). Completions still land — a missed phrasing is simply picked up at the next scheduled check instead of never.

- ⚡ **The after-reply bookkeeping now runs at the same time, not one after another** — The needs check and the shared bookkeeping request (wardrobe, afterglow, position) used to wait for each other in a line. They're independent questions, so they now go out together — on remote AI services the pause after a reply is roughly halved again on top of the fusion below.

- 🎯 **The background judges answer more reliably** — Two under-the-hood fixes to how the app talks to the AI when scoring scenes: it stopped asking the needs judge for four pieces of information nothing ever read (paying for them every turn), and it stopped applying a "don't repeat yourself" penalty to answers that are *supposed* to repeat themselves — structured scorecards with seven similar fields. Fewer garbled scorecards means fewer silently skipped updates.

- ⚡ **Faster replies when several Porch Life features are on** — After each reply, the app quietly asks the AI a few bookkeeping questions: did anything just get picked up or taken off (Pockets & Wardrobe)? where did the scene leave her standing? did the moment peak (Afterglow)? Until now each question was its own separate AI request, one after another, every single turn — three requests that each re-sent the same reply just to ask a different question about it. When two or more of those features are on, they now share **one** request that answers everything at once, so the "settling" moment after a reply is noticeably shorter and remote users pay for the reply text once instead of three times. Nothing changes about what the features do or when they're on — each still answers only to its own switch, and if you run just one of them, it works exactly as before.

- 📅 **Big fix: your story's date was quietly following the real-world calendar** — Open an older chat on a Monday and the story said Monday. Open the same chat again on the Saturday and the story said Saturday — same conversation, same scene, new date, every single time you opened it. Meanwhile the character's journal and the story recap still named the old one, so the AI would spend its thinking arguing with the app: *"Saturday August 8th, Day 1 — wait, earlier it was Monday, but the notes say Saturday."* The cause was a date that was worked out fresh each time from whatever today happened to be, and then never written down. This affects almost every chat started before the story calendar arrived — **96 of the 109 chats in my own library** were still doing it, and a chat only ever stopped once you sent a message in it, so any chat you'd only reread kept drifting forever. The date is now settled the first time it's needed and written into the chat for good. **Your chats keep the date they show today** — nothing jumps, it just stops moving. The same drift hit rerolls: rerolling an old message used to drag the entire timeline onto whatever date you were rerolling *on*, mid-conversation. Fixed too.

- ⏳ **Big fix: a character *mentioning* next week sent the story there** — "Will you post a new video next week?" is a question. The app read it as an instruction and jumped the story forward seven days — Day 1 to Day 8, in the middle of a scene, in one of my own chats. A few messages later "why wait till next week for the gym video" did it again, and that time it stuck for the rest of the conversation. Another chat lost a whole day to "even though we just woke up a few hours ago" — a line about the past, read as a command to skip to morning. It got worse from there: once the app thought you'd skipped time, it threw away the AI's own (correct) judgement that a couple of minutes had passed. So the day count marched off on its own while the journal and recap stayed in an afternoon the story had never actually left — which is exactly what the AI kept tripping over. **Time skips now only count when they're narration, not dialogue.** "*we drive for several hours*" and "(OOC: fast forward a week)" work exactly as before — I checked every time-skip phrase across my whole chat library, and 1,043 of the 1,050 were narration and are completely unaffected. The seven inside quotation marks were all mistakes.

- 🎭 **Big fix: on some local models, your character was never reaching the AI at all** — A fair number of roleplay models — mini-magnum and the many models built on it are the ones I can name — silently throw away the entire first block the app sends them. That block is the character: her description, her personality, your persona, the scenario, the example dialogue, everything. There is no error and the reply comes back looking completely normal, so there was nothing to notice — the model was simply answering as a blank assistant, and had been the whole time you'd been using it. The app now measures each model once, in the moment right after it finishes loading, and when it catches one doing this it sends the character a different way so she actually arrives. Models that were already fine are left exactly as they are. It costs three tiny requests at load and **nothing at all while you're chatting**, and if the check can't get a clear answer it leaves your model alone rather than guessing — I would rather it do nothing than "fix" something that was never broken. If your backend is still waking up when the check runs and doesn't answer, it quietly tries again a couple of times over the next few seconds rather than writing your model off for the rest of the session — that was worth fixing, because "didn't answer once" used to mean every reply for the rest of the session was still missing your character. Switching models or restarting your backend always re-checks from scratch, so a verdict can never follow the wrong model around.

- 🧭 **Fixed: characters teleported around the room** — The app keeps track of where a character physically is — sitting on the porch swing, standing at the sink — and tells the AI before every reply, so she stays put instead of blinking across the room between messages. The problem: it worked out her position *before* she'd written her reply, so if she got up and walked to the window in that reply, the app never noticed. The next message it worked it out again from scratch, and whatever it had decided last time was thrown away — so the position it kept insisting on was always one message behind the story. She'd sit down, then be told she was still standing. It now checks **after** she's spoken, reading what she actually just did, and that is what grounds her next reply. Works the same for everyone in a group chat, each keeping their own spot. **Rerolling puts her back where she was before the reply you're throwing away** — including the very first reply of a chat, which is the one that was still getting it wrong: there was no earlier message to take the position from, so each reroll quietly started from wherever the reply you'd just rejected had left her, and drifted another room every time you pressed it. Two rerolls of the same message now start from the same place, in one-to-one and group chats alike. **Heads-up on speed:** this costs one extra short AI request per reply while the Realism Engine is on — the same trade Afterglow makes. Some of it comes back, because two other requests stopped asking for the position they were getting wrong. Two follow-ups landed with it. **Her position now survives closing the app** — it was being worked out after her reply and then never written down, so reopening the chat put her back where she'd been an exchange earlier, and the whole thing teleported again; worse, whether it stuck at all quietly depended on having the Needs simulation switched on, which has nothing to do with where anyone is standing. And **the very first reply in a chat now knows where she is**: it's read from the opening scene ("she's rocking in the armchair when you walk in") instead of her starting out placed nowhere until she'd already spoken once. That works for **every** character — ones you made yourself, ones you downloaded from The Stoop, ones imported from anywhere else — and for **every member of a group chat**, each placed separately from the opening scene rather than all sharing one spot or inheriting whoever spoke first. It costs one short request at the very start of a conversation and nothing afterwards, because from her first reply onward her position comes from what she actually just did — and if you type and send before that opening request comes back, which is easy on a slow backend, it now waits for that answer instead of paying for a second one. Same when you switch the Realism Engine on partway through a chat.

- 🧠 **The app's notes now say who wrote them** — Every turn the app hands the AI a bundle of background: the story recap, the character's private journal, what she's working toward, where she is. All of it arrived looking like something *you* had typed, and one user's model spent a whole reply's worth of thinking deciding out loud that "the user included a stale recap block by mistake" — one step from a character turning round to ask you about your recap block. Those notes now introduce themselves in one short line: the app wrote them, you didn't, they need no reply — plus the rule that settles any argument. **The conversation is what actually happened, so where the notes disagree about events, the conversation wins; where they describe how she feels, the notes win, because the conversation never says.** **Being straight with you about this one:** I tested it properly — 32 turns from real chats that had genuinely confused a model before, run through Kimi 2.6 both ways. It did **not** measurably help. The model blamed the user slightly less often, but well inside the margin for error. It turned out the model wasn't confused by *tone*, it was confused because we were handing it facts that contradicted each other — which is what the three fixes below actually address. The note stays because it's correct and costs about a hundred words a reply, but I'm not going to tell you it fixed something it didn't.

- 🧠 **Big fix: your character's long-term memory could quietly stop forever** — The Journal reads your recent messages every few turns and writes what matters into the character's memory, then updates the "Where we are" recap. If one of those passes ever failed — a timeout, a backend hiccup, switching models mid-chat — it left a marker saying "resume from here", and the *next* pass tried to read everything from that marker to now. On a long chat that window kept growing, which made the next failure more likely, which made the window bigger. One bad moment and a chat's memory was finished for good, silently, with no warning anywhere. On my own test data one chat had been trying to send **4.3 million words** to the AI every few messages for weeks, failing every single time, while its recap sat frozen at message 590 of 9,488. It now reads a sensible amount no matter how far behind it fell, so a chat that stalls picks itself back up on the next pass. **Existing chats repair themselves** — nothing to press. What it can't do is go back and write the memories it missed while it was stuck.

- 🧠 **Big fix: on some models, the whole Realism Engine was silently doing nothing** — If you use a model that always shows its reasoning — Kimi K2 Thinking and friends — every single one of the app's background judges was failing. Bond, trust, mood, the needs check, quest completion: all of it, every turn, silently. Your character's feelings never moved, her mood stuck, and the needs you saw ticking down were just plain decay with nothing behind them. The cause was one line: the judges ask the model to skip its thinking (they want a short answer, not an essay), and these models refuse outright rather than comply. The app now notices the refusal, asks the only way that model accepts, and remembers — so it costs one rejected request per model, once, instead of every judge on every turn forever.

- 📖 **The story recap stops repeating what you can already read** — The "Where we are" recap exists to remember the parts of a long story that have scrolled out of the AI's view. On a shorter chat, though, the AI can already see every message — so the recap was handing it a second, older, compressed version of the same events, and models kept stopping to work out which one to believe. It's now included only when it genuinely covers something the AI can't see. Long chats are unaffected; short ones just stop arguing with themselves.

- 🍽️ **Fixed: needs could crater 35-40 points in one message** — Someone would be fine, then one reply later be starving, exhausted or desperate for a bathroom, sometimes several at once. It was a feedback loop: the app tells the AI how the character feels ("sharp, gnawing hunger cramps"), the AI writes her clutching her stomach — and then the part that scores the scene read *that* and decided she'd got hungrier still. The worse she felt, the more vividly it was written, the harder the next hit. **Describing a feeling is no longer counted as the feeling getting worse.** What a single scene can take out of a need is now capped per need, and the caps are deliberately different: drinking a soda still hits her bladder about three times harder than a normal turn would, a soaking still ruins her hygiene, but nothing drops off a cliff any more. Eating, sleeping, bathing and using the bathroom still fill her right up in one go, exactly as before. The Needs strength slider still widens everything, and every setting is calmer than it was.

- 🔌 **Big fix: several features did nothing at all unless the Realism Engine was on** — Pockets & Wardrobe, Likes & Dislikes, Ambitions, Promises, the "you've been away" note and the story clock's own time and weather all travel to the AI inside one shared block of text — and that whole block was being skipped whenever the Realism Engine was off. Every one of those features says plainly in Settings that it doesn't need the engine, and their switches, sidebars and displays all worked, so there was nothing to see. The AI just never got told. Worst hit: **Pockets & Wardrobe**, which spends an AI request every turn — with the engine off you were paying for it and the character was never told what she was wearing — and the **story clock's stand-alone mode**, which exists purely to keep time running without the engine and was doing so entirely in private. All of them reach the AI now. Nothing changes if you run with the engine on.

- 🎭 **Fixed: characters you downloaded ignored your Settings defaults** — Turn on Realism, Afterglow or Chaos Mode as a global default, open a character card you'd downloaded from Chub (or exported from another app), and none of it applied. The app was only reading your defaults for characters that already had Front Porch settings saved on them — which downloaded cards, by definition, don't. That was backwards: those are exactly the cards the defaults exist for. Your own characters are unaffected.

- 🧥 **Group chats now show what each character is wearing and carrying** — Pockets & Wardrobe worked in group chats the whole time — the AI knew, the phone/web app showed it — but the desktop sidebar only ever displayed it in one-to-one chats, so you couldn't see it and couldn't correct a wrong entry. Each member's card now shows their wardrobe with the same tap-to-remove as a solo chat.

- 🔎 **New: you can turn off "notice new characters"** — Every few messages the app checks whether a new named character has appeared in the story and offers to bring them in. Handy, and easy to find intrusive. There's now a switch for it in **Settings → Porch Life → Notice new characters**, on by default. Turn it off and it stops asking — you can still invite someone in yourself whenever you like. On the web/mobile app too.

- 🎲 **Fixed: starting a new chat inside a group ignored your Chaos Mode default** — Changing the global Chaos setting while a group was open, then starting a fresh chat in it, kept the old setting. One-to-one chats always got this right.

- 🔞 **A character with intimate preferences is now tagged 18+ automatically, everywhere** — If you've written 18+ tastes onto a character, that character is 18+ no matter how tame the rest of the card reads, and whether they get labelled that way is no longer down to remembering to tick a box. Pick one to share — from the app or from the website — and I tag it for you, lock the switch, and put a notice above it saying why and what to do instead: if you'd rather share that character as SFW, remove the intimate preferences from the card, re-export, and share the new file. The Stoop enforces it on its side too, so an older version of the app can't publish one as safe-for-work by not knowing the rule exists. It looks inside group casts as well — one member with intimate preferences makes the whole group card 18+, because that member is who you'd be downloading.

- 🧭 **The Stoop website now shows a character's ambitions, likes and wardrobe too** — Browsing a card on [the website](https://hub.frontporchai.app) showed you their description, personality, scenario and greeting, and stopped there — while the app and the phone/web version had already started showing what they're chasing, what they're drawn to and put off by, and what they're wearing and carrying before the first message. The website was the odd one out; it isn't now. Same collapsible sections, same order, in the same places you'd expect them. Group casts show them per member, so you can see who you're actually getting before you download the whole family.

- 👗 **Fixed: a character's wardrobe was invisible in the sidebar until you sent your first message** — Dress a character, open a chat with her, and the Pockets & Wardrobe section simply wasn't there. It appeared only after you typed something — which is the worst possible timing, because opening the chat is exactly when you go to check the wardrobe saved. (It did save; it just wasn't being shown yet.) She now arrives dressed, on the greeting, in one-to-one chats and group chats alike.

- 🧥 **Fixed: wardrobe and pocket items you typed onto a character were thrown away when you pressed Save** — Dressing a character in the editor — what she's wearing, what she's carrying, and her ambitions, likes and dislikes too — saved successfully, wrote the file, and lost every word of it. There was no error and nothing looked wrong; reopen the character and the chips were simply gone. The save only wrote that part of the card if the Realism Engine was on for that character or you'd touched one of the engine's own sliders, and none of those boxes count as either. It now saves whenever you've actually written something, which is the only rule that can't be forgotten the next time a box is added.

- 🎲 **Chaos Mode has a global switch at last** — Chaos could only ever be turned on one chat at a time, forever, and the bottom of the Porch Life tab had a little note apologising for it. It's now a row like everything else in **Settings → Porch Life**, it depends on nothing, and it's off unless you turn it on. Switch it on and new chats and groups start with Chaos running; any single chat can still overrule it from its sidebar, same as before. The apologetic note is gone. On the web/mobile app too.

- 💗 **Characters act on what they're into, instead of just quietly having preferences** — The 18+ tastes you write on a card were only ever a footnote in the AI's instructions: "relevant when the scene is already there." It said *when* they applied and never what to do about them, so a character never once asked for anything or turned anything down — while the engine was already, silently, moving bond and trust based on whether a scene hit them. She was rewarding and penalising you over things she'd never say out loud. Now she can say them: she asks for what she wants **in her own voice** — a dominant character presses, a soft-spoken one hints or waits to be noticed — and she declines what she isn't interested in rather than going along with it. Refuse something she wanted and it shows in her mood afterwards, sharper or quieter as fits who she is, and the engine scores that refusal as the real moment it is. New switch: **Settings → Porch Life → After Dark → Acts on desires**, off unless you turn it on, and it needs the Realism Engine because that's what judges the answer she gets. **Costs nothing** — no extra AI request, just two more lines in the prompt.
